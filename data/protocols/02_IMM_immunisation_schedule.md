# IMM — Immunisation Schedule (Universal Immunization Programme)

> Simplified for the TalentAP hackathon from public Government of India guidance. Not for clinical use.
> Local terms: vaccine = *tika / teeka*; immunisation session = *tikakaran diwas*; drops = *polio ki boond*; injection = *sui*.

## IMM-1 General rules

### IMM-1.1 Sessions, delays and gaps
- In Chandrapuri Block, routine immunisation sessions are held every **Wednesday and Saturday** at the sub-centre and Anganwadi centres (block arrangement).
- Keep a **minimum gap of 4 weeks** between two doses of the same vaccine (Penta, OPV, RVV). There is no maximum gap: if a child comes late, **do not restart the series** — give the next due dose.
- A child can be vaccinated even if she/he has diarrhoea, mild fever or malnutrition.
- After any vaccine, the child (or woman) should **wait 30 minutes** at the session site.

### IMM-1.2 Due list and tracking
Before each session, the ASHA/ANM prepares a due list of children and pregnant women from the register (see VIS document) and mobilises them. Record every dose with its date on the MCP card and in the register.

## IMM-2 Schedule for infants and children

### IMM-2.1 At birth
| Vaccine | When | Upper limit |
|---|---|---|
| BCG | At birth or as early as possible | till one year of age |
| Hepatitis B birth dose (HepB-0) | At birth | within 24 hours of birth |
| OPV-0 (oral polio, zero dose) | At birth | within the first 15 days |

### IMM-2.2 At 6, 10 and 14 weeks
| Age | Vaccines |
|---|---|
| 6 weeks | OPV-1, Penta-1, RVV-1, fIPV-1, PCV-1 |
| 10 weeks | OPV-2, Penta-2, RVV-2 |
| 14 weeks | OPV-3, Penta-3, RVV-3, fIPV-2, PCV-2 |

- Penta = pentavalent vaccine; RVV = rotavirus vaccine (drops); fIPV = fractional injectable polio vaccine (intradermal, 0.1 ml); PCV = pneumococcal conjugate vaccine.
- OPV can be given till 5 years of age.
- Penta and RVV can be given **till one year of age**. fIPV-1 and fIPV-2 are also given only up to 1 year. If a child over 1 year still has missing Penta or RVV doses, inform the ANM/MO, who decides the catch-up.
- PCV is not started after 1 year. If the child received at least one PCV dose before the first birthday, the remaining PCV doses are completed as early as possible, at least 2 months apart, within the "before 2 years" complete-immunisation window.
- RVV and PCV are now given in all states.

### IMM-2.3 At 9–12 months
- **MR-1** (measles-rubella) at **9 completed months** to 12 months; can be given till 5 years of age.
- **fIPV-3** (third fractional IPV dose) at **9 months**, given together with MR-1 (added to the schedule from 1 January 2023; children who received MR-1 by 31 December 2022 are not called again for fIPV-3).
- **PCV booster (PCV-B)** at 9–12 months (if delayed, completed before 2 years of age — see IMM-2.2).
- **Vitamin A 1st dose** (1 lakh IU, 1 ml) at 9 completed months with MR-1.
- JE-1 only in JE-endemic districts.

### IMM-2.4 At 16–24 months
- **MR-2**, **DPT booster-1 (DPT-B1)**, **OPV booster (OPV-B)**; JE-2 only in endemic districts.
- Give the OPV/DPT booster at least **6 months after OPV-3/Penta-3**. DPT boosters can be given till **7 years of age**.
- **Vitamin A 2nd dose** (2 lakh IU, 2 ml) at 16–18 months, then one dose **every 6 months up to 5 years** of age (9 doses in all).
- If fIPV-3 was missed at 9 months, it can be given with MR-2 at 16–23 months of age, keeping at least 8 weeks after fIPV-2. It is not given after 23 completed months (i.e. not after the second birthday).
- Chandrapuri is **not a JE-endemic district**, so JE vaccine is not given here.

### IMM-2.5 Older children and adolescents
- **DPT booster-2 (DPT-B2)** at **5–6 years**.
- **Td** at **10 years** and at **16 years**.
- **HPV vaccine:** girls who have completed 14 years but not yet 15 years — a **single dose**, given at government health facilities (with cold chain) on routine immunisation days; nationwide roll-out began on 28 February 2026; registration on U-WIN.

### IMM-2.6 Minimum gaps between doses
| Vaccine | Minimum gap between doses |
|---|---|
| Penta, OPV, RVV | 4 weeks |
| PCV | 2 months |
| fIPV | 8 weeks |
| MR-1 to MR-2 (if delayed) | 4 weeks |
| OPV-3/Penta-3 to booster | 6 months |

## IMM-3 After vaccination (AEFI)

### IMM-3.1 Common mild reactions
- Pain, swelling or redness at the injection site, and fever, can occur in about 10% of children (with DPT-containing vaccines in up to half of children). They settle on their own; a cold cloth on the injection site helps. Any medicine is as advised by the ANM/MO.
- BCG: a small swelling appears at the site, may become a small sore, and heals leaving a scar — this is normal.
- MR: mild fever, rash or red eyes can occur in 5–15% of children.
- **Exception:** in a baby **under 2 months** (e.g. after the 6-week vaccines), any fever is a danger sign — follow ESC-4.3.

### IMM-3.2 Reactions to report and refer
Report **immediately** to the ANM (by phone or in person) any high fever, allergic reaction, or **convulsions after immunisation**, and other serious events: persistent screaming, the child becoming limp or unresponsive, difficulty in breathing or swelling of face (anaphylaxis), a large abscess at the injection site, or hospitalisation/death after vaccination. A child with convulsions, breathing difficulty or unresponsiveness after a vaccine must be referred immediately (ESC-5.6).

## IMM-4 Pregnant women

### IMM-4.1 Td in pregnancy
| Dose | When |
|---|---|
| Td-1 | Early in pregnancy (at registration) |
| Td-2 | 4 weeks after Td-1 |
| Td-Booster | If she received 2 TT/Td doses in a pregnancy within the last 3 years — then give only one booster dose |

- Td (tetanus and adult diphtheria) replaced TT in the programme in **2018**. Earlier TT doses remain valid; do not restart the series.
- Give Td-2 or the booster preferably before 36 weeks of pregnancy.
