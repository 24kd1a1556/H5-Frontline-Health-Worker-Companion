# ANC — Antenatal Care (Pregnancy Check-ups)

> Simplified for the TalentAP hackathon from public Government of India guidance. Not for clinical use.
> Local terms: pregnancy = *garbh / pet se*; ANC check-up = *jaanch*; LMP = *aakhri mahwari ki tareekh*; IFA = *lal goli / aayaran ki goli*; MCP card = *Mamta card / jachcha-bachcha card*.

## ANC-1 Registration and check-up schedule

### ANC-1.1 Early registration and EDD
- Register every pregnancy as early as possible — **within 12 weeks**, preferably as soon as pregnancy is suspected. Give the woman her MCP card and fill it at every contact.
- Ask the first day of her last menstrual period (LMP).
- Expected date of delivery (EDD): the MoHFW rule is LMP + 9 months + 7 days, which is about 280 days (40 weeks). In the Chandrapuri register and visit list the EDD is always calculated as **LMP + 280 days**. Example: LMP 2026-03-01 → EDD (LMP + 280 days) 2026-12-06.
- Weeks of pregnancy = days since LMP ÷ 7.

### ANC-1.2 Minimum four ANC check-ups
Every pregnant woman should have **at least four ANC check-ups**. This is only a minimum; more visits may be needed.

| Check-up | When |
|---|---|
| 1st | Within 12 weeks (as soon as pregnancy is suspected) |
| 2nd | Between 14 and 26 weeks |
| 3rd | Between 28 and 34 weeks |
| 4th | Between 36 weeks and term |

If a check-up is missed, bring the woman for her next check-up as soon as possible; do not wait for the next window.

### ANC-1.3 PMSMA day (9th of every month)
Under Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA), a check-up by a doctor/specialist is held at government health facilities on the **9th of every month**, for women in the **second or third trimester**. Mobilise every pregnant woman in the second or third trimester to attend at least one PMSMA check-up. High-risk pregnancies found there are tracked.

### ANC-1.4 What is checked at an ANC check-up
At each check-up the ANM checks: **weight**, **blood pressure (BP)**, **haemoglobin (Hb)**, **urine for protein and sugar**, and does an abdominal examination for growth of the baby. She also gives IFA, calcium, Td and deworming as due (ANC-2), and counsels on diet, rest, danger signs (ESC-2) and birth preparedness (ANC-5).

## ANC-2 Supplements, deworming and Td in pregnancy

### ANC-2.1 Folic acid before and in early pregnancy
**400 mcg folic acid** tablet daily during the period before pregnancy (when planning a pregnancy) and up to the end of the **first trimester**.

### ANC-2.2 IFA tablets (iron and folic acid)
- **One Red IFA tablet daily for 180 days**, starting from the **second trimester** of pregnancy. Each tablet contains **60 mg elemental iron + 500 mcg folic acid**.
- After delivery: one Red IFA tablet daily for **180 days** (up to 6 months after delivery).
- Do not take IFA and calcium tablets together: take calcium with meals, and IFA at bedtime or at least two hours after a meal.
- IFA is held during acute illness such as fever, diarrhoea or pneumonia; restart after the illness. For women with thalassemia major or sickle cell disease, iron is decided by the MO.
- Anaemic women get treatment doses (ANC-3.2).

### ANC-2.3 Calcium tablets
- Each tablet: **500 mg elemental calcium + 250 IU vitamin D3**.
- **Twice a day** (total **1 g calcium per day**), starting from **14 weeks of pregnancy** up to **6 months after delivery**.
- Take with meals, never on an empty stomach. Do not take calcium and IFA tablets together, because calcium reduces iron absorption.

### ANC-2.4 Deworming (albendazole) in pregnancy
- **Single dose of albendazole 400 mg** (1 tablet).
- Give it **after the first trimester**, preferably during the second trimester. **Do not give in the first trimester.**

### ANC-2.5 Td vaccine in pregnancy
Td-1 early in pregnancy, Td-2 **4 weeks after Td-1**; only one Td-Booster if she received 2 TT/Td doses in a pregnancy within the last 3 years. Full rule: IMM-4.1.

## ANC-3 Anaemia in pregnancy (khoon ki kami)

### ANC-3.1 Hb cut-offs in pregnancy
Anaemia Mukt Bharat (2026) uses these haemoglobin (Hb) cut-offs, in g/dL:

| Trimester | No anaemia | Mild | Moderate | Severe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st and 3rd trimester | 11.0 or more | 10.0–10.9 | 7.0–9.9 | below 7.0 |
| 2nd trimester | 10.5 or more | 9.5–10.4 | 7.0–9.4 | below 7.0 |

Signs that suggest anaemia: pallor of the inner eyelids, tongue, palms and nails; tiredness; breathlessness on work.

### ANC-3.2 What to do for anaemia
- **Mild or moderate anaemia:** treatment as advised by the ANM/MO is **2 Red IFA tablets daily for 1 month**, then re-test Hb. If Hb has not improved, refer to the FRU/District Hospital.
- **Severe anaemia (Hb below 7 g/dL):** refer the pregnant woman to a higher facility (FRU/District Hospital) — this is a danger sign (ESC-2.5).
- Counsel on iron-rich food (green leafy vegetables, pulses, jaggery, meat/eggs if eaten) and on taking IFA daily.

## ANC-4 High blood pressure in pregnancy

### ANC-4.1 BP 140/90 or more
- Hypertension in pregnancy = systolic BP **140 mmHg or more** and/or diastolic BP **90 mmHg or more** (confirmed on two readings at least 4 hours apart by the ANM/MO).
- A pregnant woman with BP 140/90 or more is a **high-risk pregnancy**: refer her to the Medical Officer the same day; do not wait for the next ANC check-up (ESC-2.3).

### ANC-4.2 Pre-eclampsia and eclampsia
- **Pre-eclampsia** = high BP with protein in the urine. Warning signs: severe headache, blurring of vision, swelling of face/hands or all over the body, pain in the upper abdomen, reduced urine.
- **Eclampsia** = high BP with protein in urine **and convulsions (fits / jhatke)**.
- Any of these is a danger sign: refer immediately (ESC-2.3, ESC-2.2).
- Mild swelling of the feet alone, with normal BP and no other sign, is common in late pregnancy — check BP at the next contact and counsel; it is not by itself a danger sign.

## ANC-5 High-risk pregnancy, birth preparedness and free services

### ANC-5.1 High-risk pregnancy
Some pregnancies need extra care and delivery at a hospital with a doctor (FRU). Conditions tracked as high-risk under PMSMA include: severe anaemia, pregnancy-induced hypertension, gestational diabetes, hypothyroidism, previous caesarean section (LSCS), bad obstetric history or history of stillbirth, twins/multiple pregnancy, teenage pregnancy, elderly or early primi, grand multipara, short stature, cephalo-pelvic disproportion, abnormal foetal heart rate, congenital malformation, negative (Rh) blood group, HIV, syphilis, RTI/STI, hepatitis B, tuberculosis, malaria and high fever. Make sure these women attend PMSMA (ANC-1.3) and plan delivery at a facility that can manage them.

### ANC-5.2 Birth preparedness
Plan in advance: the place of delivery (institutional delivery), transport, money, a companion, and a blood donor. Keep the MCP card ready. Know the ambulance numbers: **102** (free transport for pregnant women and sick infants in most states) and **108** (emergency).

### ANC-5.3 JSSK free services
Under Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram (JSSK), pregnant women delivering in government facilities get free: delivery (including caesarean section), drugs, diagnostics, diet and blood; **free transport from home to the health institution**, between facilities in case of referral, and **drop back home after 48 hours** of stay. Sick infants up to 1 year also get free treatment and transport.
