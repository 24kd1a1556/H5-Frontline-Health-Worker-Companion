# NCD — Hypertension and Diabetes: Screening and Follow-up

> Simplified for the TalentAP hackathon from public Government of India guidance. Not for clinical use.
> Local terms: high BP = *BP high / uchch raktchaap*; diabetes = *sugar / madhumeh*; medicine = *dawai*; salt = *namak*.

## NCD-1 Screening

### NCD-1.1 Who is screened and how often
- All women and men aged **30 years and above** are screened for hypertension and diabetes **once a year**, whatever their CBAC score.
- The ASHA fills the **CBAC form** (Community Based Assessment Checklist). A **score above 4** means the person may be at higher risk and should be **prioritised** for screening.

### NCD-1.2 BP screening and referral
- Hypertension = systolic BP **140 mmHg or more** and/or diastolic BP **90 mmHg or more**, found on **two different occasions**.
- A person whose screening BP is 140/90 or more is referred to the **Medical Officer** (PHC / Ayushman Arogya Mandir) to confirm the diagnosis and start treatment. This is a routine referral, not an emergency — unless the BP is 180/110 or more (then escalate: same-day referral, ESC-6.4) or danger signs are present (ESC-6).
- For the MO's information: 140–159 / 90–99 → check again on another day; 160–179 / 100–109 → second reading the same day and start treatment the same day; **180 or more / 110 or more** → start treatment and refer to a specialist.

### NCD-1.3 Blood sugar screening and referral
- A random blood sugar by glucostrip of **140 mg/dl or more** is a suspected diabetes case → refer to the **Medical Officer** for confirmation.
- Diagnosis is made by the MO: fasting **126 mg/dl or more**, or 2-hour post-meal 200 mg/dl or more, or HbA1c 6.5% or more, or random 200 mg/dl or more with symptoms of diabetes.

## NCD-2 Follow-up of known patients

### NCD-2.1 Monthly follow-up
- The ASHA visits every known hypertension/diabetes patient **every month** to check that medicines are being taken, support diet and lifestyle change, and encourage the patient to get **BP and blood sugar measured every month**.
- Medicines are **refilled monthly** at the PHC / Ayushman Arogya Mandir.
- If BP is still **140/90 or more**, or sugar stays high, the condition is not controlled → refer to the Medical Officer for review. Exception: if BP is **180/110 or more**, this is an escalation — refer the same day (ESC-6.4).
- Each patient should be reviewed by a doctor/specialist **at least once a year**.

### NCD-2.2 Medicines
- Patients must **not stop their medicines** on their own, even when they feel well or the reading is normal.
- The ASHA does **not** start, stop or change the dose of any BP or diabetes medicine or insulin — that is decided only by the Medical Officer (SCP-1.2).

### NCD-2.3 Low blood sugar in diabetes patients
- Low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia) = **below 70 mg/dl**.
- Signs: tremors, sweating, nervousness, irritability, confusion, fast heartbeat, dizziness, hunger, blurred vision, headache, weakness; in severe cases fits or unconsciousness.
- Action: give **sugar, misri or a toffee** at once if the person is awake. Advise diabetes patients to always carry sugar, misri or toffee. Unconscious, fits or not improving → refer immediately (ESC-6.3).

## NCD-3 Healthy lifestyle advice

### NCD-3.1 Lifestyle counselling
- Salt: a **maximum of 1 teaspoon (5 g) of salt** for the whole day, including salt in cooking; avoid pickles, papad and packaged salty snacks.
- Physical activity: **at least 30 minutes** of regular activity for **at least 5 days a week** (150 minutes a week).
- Stop tobacco (bidi, cigarette, gutkha, khaini) and avoid alcohol.
- Eat more fruits, vegetables and whole grains; keep a healthy weight.
