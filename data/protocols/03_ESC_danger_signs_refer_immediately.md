# ESC — Danger Signs: Refer Immediately

> Simplified for the TalentAP hackathon from public Government of India guidance. Not for clinical use.
> Local terms: fits = *jhatke / mirgi jaisa daura*; bleeding = *khoon aana*; unconscious = *behosh*; breathing fast = *saans tez chalna*; chest indrawing = *pasli chalna*; swelling = *sujan*; fever = *bukhar*.

## ESC-1 The escalation rule

### ESC-1.1 Refer immediately
If **even one** danger sign in this document is present, **refer immediately** — the same day, without delay. Do not wait, do not try home treatment first (except the first-aid steps in ESC-1.3), and do not answer the question as a routine one.
- Arrange transport at once: call **108** (emergency ambulance) or **102** (free transport for pregnant women and sick infants under JSSK).
- Take the person to the nearest facility that can manage the problem (PHC/CHC/FRU, or Chandrapuri District Hospital); for newborns the SNCU; for severely malnourished children the NRC (but if a general danger sign is present, the nearest hospital first).
- Inform the ANM and the Medical Officer by phone. Go with the family if possible and carry the MCP card / referral slip.

### ESC-1.2 Check danger signs first
Danger signs are checked **before** anything else. If you are not sure whether a danger sign is present, treat it as present and refer. A sign that has stopped (for example, a fit that is over, or bleeding that has reduced) still needs referral.

### ESC-1.3 While arranging referral
- Newborn: keep the baby warm by skin-to-skin contact with the mother, covered with a cloth, and continue breastfeeding if the baby can suck.
- Person with low sugar who is awake and can swallow: give sugar, misri or a toffee at once (ESC-6.3).
- Do not give anything by mouth to a person who is unconscious or having fits.

### ESC-1.4 Other life-threatening emergencies
Any other emergency that threatens life — for example snakebite, poisoning (pesticide, kerosene or medicines swallowed), severe burns, serious injury, drowning, a person who is unconscious, very drowsy or confused, a diabetes patient whose glucometer shows a very high reading or "HI" together with vomiting, drowsiness or confusion, or someone talking of suicide — also needs you to refer immediately: call 108. Never refuse such a question just because the condition is not covered in these protocols.

## ESC-2 Pregnancy and labour

### ESC-2.1 Bleeding in pregnancy
**Any bleeding from the vagina during pregnancy** (first, second or third trimester), and excessive bleeding during delivery.

### ESC-2.2 Fits or unconsciousness
**Convulsions (fits / jhatke)** or loss of consciousness in a pregnant woman (possible eclampsia).

### ESC-2.3 High blood pressure and pre-eclampsia signs
- **BP 140/90 or more** in a pregnant woman (ANC-4.1).
- **Severe headache, blurring of vision**, or **swelling of the face and hands / swelling all over the body**, or reduced urine with high BP — signs of pre-eclampsia.

### ESC-2.4 High fever
**High fever during pregnancy or within one month of delivery** — temperature **more than 38 °C** (100.4 °F).

### ESC-2.5 Severe anaemia or breathlessness
- **Hb below 7 g/dL** (severe anaemia), or **severe pallor with or without breathlessness**.
- **Fast or difficult breathing**, or breathlessness at rest.

### ESC-2.6 Severe abdominal pain or vomiting
**Continuous severe abdominal pain**, or **excessive vomiting** — unable to take anything by mouth.

### ESC-2.7 Baby's movements reduced
**Decreased or absent foetal movements** (the baby in the womb is moving less than usual or not at all).

### ESC-2.8 Water bag, prolonged labour, foul discharge
- **Bursting of the water bag** (leaking watery fluid) **without labour pains**, or before 37 weeks.
- **Labour pain for more than 12 hours.**
- Water bag burst (membranes ruptured) **for more than 18 hours**.
- In labour: **malpresentation** (a hand, foot or cord coming first), **twins / multiple pregnancy**, or **placenta not delivered within 30 minutes** after the birth of the baby.
- **Foul-smelling discharge** from the vagina.

## ESC-3 Mother after delivery (up to 42 days)

### ESC-3.1 Heavy bleeding after delivery
**Heavy bleeding**: a pad is soaked in **less than 5 minutes**, or **more than 5 pads** are changed in a day.

### ESC-3.2 Fever or foul-smelling discharge after delivery
**High fever within one month of delivery** (temperature more than 38 °C), or **foul-smelling discharge with fever**. (The HBNC home-visit form uses 102 °F for fever alone; this protocol uses the stricter 38 °C from the MCP card and the 2010 ANC guidelines.)

### ESC-3.3 Fits, abnormal behaviour or severe headache after delivery
**Fits**, speaking or behaving abnormally, loss of consciousness, or severe headache with blurring of vision.

### ESC-3.4 Breathlessness, pallor or abdominal pain after delivery
After delivery (up to 42 days): **fast or difficult breathing** or **breathlessness at rest**, **severe pallor** (severe anaemia), or **continuous severe abdominal pain**.

## ESC-4 Newborn and young infant (up to 2 months)

### ESC-4.1 Feeding, cry and activity
- **Feeding less or stopped / unable to suck** at the breast.
- **Cry weak or stopped.**
- **All limbs limp**, or the baby is **lethargic or unconscious**.
- **Convulsions (fits).**

### ESC-4.2 Breathing
- Breathing **60 per minute or more** (count for one full minute).
- **Chest indrawing**, difficulty in breathing, or blue tongue and lips.

### ESC-4.3 Temperature
- Baby has fever: feels hot, or temperature **more than 99 °F (37.2 °C)** on the HBNC thermometer check.
- Baby is **cold to touch**, or temperature **below 95.9 °F (35.5 °C)**.
- The newborn fever cut-off (more than 99 °F, from the HBNC home-visit form) is deliberately lower than the 37.5 °C used for older children in CHD-2.1.

### ESC-4.4 Other newborn danger signs
- **Pus or redness of the umbilicus**, or bleeding from the cord.
- **10 or more skin pustules**, or a big boil.
- **Jaundice on the first day**, **yellow palms and soles**, or jaundice that persists after 14 days.
- **Distended abdomen**, or the baby vomits often.
- **Blood in the stool.**
- **Weight less than 1.8 kg** (refer to SNCU).

## ESC-5 Child 2 months to 5 years

### ESC-5.1 General danger signs
- **Not able to drink or breastfeed.**
- **Vomits everything.**
- **Convulsions (fits)** now or during this illness.
- **Lethargic or unconscious.**

### ESC-5.2 Cough with fast breathing or chest indrawing
Cough or difficult breathing **with fast breathing** (**50 per minute or more** at 2–12 months; **40 per minute or more** at 12 months–5 years), **chest indrawing**, or **stridor** (harsh noise on breathing in) in a calm child.

### ESC-5.3 Diarrhoea danger signs
- **Severe dehydration** — two of: lethargic or unconscious; sunken eyes; not able to drink or drinking poorly; skin pinch goes back very slowly (longer than 2 seconds).
- **Blood in the stools.**
- **Diarrhoea for 14 days or more.**
- Diarrhoea in a child with **severe malnutrition** (refer to NRC even without dehydration).

### ESC-5.4 Fever danger signs
- Fever with **any general danger sign** (ESC-5.1) or a **stiff neck**.
- **Fever every day for more than 7 days.**

### ESC-5.5 Severe acute malnutrition with complications
Refer immediately to the **NRC** if a child has **oedema of both feet**, or SAM (MUAC below 11.5 cm) together with any of: poor appetite (fails the appetite test), visible severe wasting, severe palmar pallor, lethargy or drowsiness, continual irritability, any breathing difficulty, or signs of severe dehydration. Any infant under 6 months who is **visibly wasted, has oedema, or is too weak to suckle** must also be referred immediately.

### ESC-5.6 Serious reaction after vaccination
**Convulsions**, breathing difficulty or swelling of the face, the child becoming **limp or unresponsive**, or persistent screaming after a vaccine. Refer immediately and report to the ANM at once (IMM-3.2).

### ESC-5.7 Severe palmar pallor
**Severe palmar pallor** (palms very pale/white) in a child — possible severe anaemia.

## ESC-6 Adults with hypertension or diabetes

### ESC-6.1 Heart attack signs
**Any pain, pressure or tightness in the centre of the chest**, especially with sweating, shortness of breath, nausea, or pain spreading to the jaw, neck, arms, shoulders or back, or unconsciousness → refer immediately to the CHC/hospital (call 108). Do not wait to see whether the pain passes. People with diabetes may have a heart attack with little or no pain.

### ESC-6.2 Stroke signs (BE FAST)
**Sudden weakness, paralysis or numbness of the face, arm or leg on one side**, **loss of speech or difficulty speaking**, sudden dimness or loss of vision, sudden dizziness, unsteadiness or falls, or a sudden severe headache or loss of consciousness → refer immediately. BE FAST = Balance, Eyes, Face, Arm, Speech, Time.

### ESC-6.3 Low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia)
A diabetes patient with sweating, tremors, confusion, fast heartbeat, dizziness or blurred vision may have low sugar (**below 70 mg/dl**). If awake and able to swallow, give **sugar, misri or a toffee at once** (first aid, ESC-1.3), then send the person to the Medical Officer the same day, because low sugar in a person on diabetes medicines can come back. If the person is **unconscious, having fits, or does not improve** after the sugar, refer immediately (call 108).

### ESC-6.4 Very high blood pressure
**BP 180/110 or more** (systolic 180 or more, or diastolic 110 or more), even without any complaint, is an escalation: refer the person to the Medical Officer the same day — the MO starts treatment and refers to a specialist (NCD-1.2). If it comes with severe headache, chest pain, breathlessness, confusion or weakness of one side, call 108 and refer immediately (ESC-6.1, ESC-6.2).
