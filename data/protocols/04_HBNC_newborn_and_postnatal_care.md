# HBNC — Home-Based Newborn Care and Postnatal Care

> Simplified for the TalentAP hackathon from public Government of India guidance. Not for clinical use.
> Local terms: newborn = *naya bachcha / navjaat*; breastfeeding = *maa ka doodh pilana*; umbilical cord = *naal*; jaundice = *peeliya*; low weight = *kam wajan*.

## HBNC-1 Home visits after birth

### HBNC-1.1 HBNC visit schedule
| Place of delivery | Number of home visits | Visit days |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional delivery | 6 visits | Days 3, 7, 14, 21, 28 and 42 |
| Home delivery | 7 visits | Days 1, 3, 7, 14, 21, 28 and 42 |

- The day of birth is day 1. For a baby born at home, the first visit is on the day of birth.
- After a caesarean delivery, visits start from day 7 (because mother and baby stay longer in hospital).
- At every visit check both the **baby** (HBNC-1.3) and the **mother** (HBNC-3).

### HBNC-1.2 Low birth weight babies
- **Low birth weight (LBW) = birth weight less than 2.5 kg.** These babies need special care: extra warmth (skin-to-skin), more frequent breastfeeding, and three extra home visits (MCP card).
- A baby weighing **less than 1.8 kg** must be referred to the SNCU (sick newborn care unit) — see ESC-4.4.
- LBW babies and babies discharged from SNCU get follow-up visits in the 3rd, 6th, 9th and 12th month (NHSRC HBNC/HBYC handbook).

### HBNC-1.3 What to check in the baby at each visit
Feeding (is the baby sucking well?), cry, activity (is the baby limp?), breathing rate (count for one full minute) and chest indrawing, temperature, umbilical cord (pus, redness, bleeding), skin (pustules), eyes, jaundice (yellow skin, palms, soles), stools and vomiting, weight. Any danger sign → refer immediately (ESC-4).

## HBNC-2 Essential newborn care advice

### HBNC-2.1 Breastfeeding
- Start breastfeeding **within 1 hour of birth**.
- **Exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months**: only breast milk — no water, honey, ghutti, animal milk or anything else.
- Feed on demand, day and night; give the first yellow milk (colostrum).

### HBNC-2.2 Keeping the baby warm and bathing
- Keep the baby warm: skin-to-skin contact with the mother (Kangaroo Mother Care), cover the head, hands and feet, keep the baby with the mother.
- If the baby's temperature is below 97 °F, warm the baby by skin-to-skin contact and check again after about 1 hour; if it is still below 97 °F, refer. A baby who is cold to touch, or whose temperature is below 95.9 °F (35.5 °C), is in danger (ESC-4.3).
- **Do not bathe the baby for the first 48 hours.** Do not bathe a low birth weight baby until its weight is 2000 g; clean with a soft, warm, wet cloth instead.

### HBNC-2.3 Cord care
- **Do not apply anything on the cord** (no oil, ash, cow dung, powder or ointment). Keep the cord and umbilicus clean and dry.

## HBNC-3 Care of the mother after delivery (postnatal care)

### HBNC-3.1 Mother's check and supplements
- At each visit ask about bleeding (how many pads changed in a day), fever, foul-smelling discharge, pain, breast problems, fits or abnormal talk. Danger signs → refer immediately (ESC-3).
- Continue **one Red IFA tablet daily for 180 days** after delivery and **calcium (two tablets a day) up to 6 months** after delivery (ANC-2.2, ANC-2.3).
- Counsel on nutritious diet, rest, breastfeeding, and family planning methods (refer to ANM for a method).

## HBNC-4 Home-Based Young Child Care (HBYC)

### HBNC-4.1 HBYC visits
After HBNC ends, the ASHA makes **five HBYC home visits in the 3rd, 6th, 9th, 12th and 15th month** of the child's life — to check growth, immunisation, exclusive breastfeeding until 6 months, start of complementary feeding at 6 months, ORS and zinc use, and play/communication.
